# 01 one hand

**Placement:** left chest, 9.5 cm wide, inner edge 4 cm off centre, top 17 cm under the shoulder point.
**Inks:** violet `#5B1F8E`, red `#DC3D4B`, amber `#F7C24D`, cream `#F6EFD2` (the back's own bands).
**Words:** none.

## Brief

For the crowd at the December shows who bought the heat-camera back: they want something on the front they can wear
alone and that still belongs to that night. The idea is the back's one gesture, lifted out: a single hand up for the
drop, palm to the stage, as the stage's camera sees it. It is cut by hand as a reduction linocut, never a smooth
thermal gradient: four flat bands, each its own drawn shape, a little off register. The cool skin rim is red, the
warm body amber, the hot core cream (the heel of the palm, the base of the fingers), the fingertips cooler than the
palm, each finger its own temperature. The December jacket sleeve pushed down to the wrist is cold violet, with a red
lip where the heat leaks out of it. Palm creases, one knuckle per finger, a few echo cuts and the sleeve's folds are
gouged out so the black shirt shows through, the way a lino block prints. The block stops on a ragged cut edge under
the sleeve.

Rules: no gradient, no glow, no camera interface, no noise; binary alpha; marks at least 1 mm; four inks.

## How it pairs with the back

The back is the crowd with hands up for the drop; the front is one of those hands, close enough to see the creases.
Alone, it reads as a raised hand in hot colours: a hello, a "this one", the drop.

## Weak spots

The critic (one pass) found the first hand read as a "stop" emoji: a thumb sticking out like a sixth finger, a small
palm with rake-tine fingers, every band an even inset of the one outside it, gouges like pen lines. The rework rebuilt
the hand on real proportions (palm as wide as long, webbing, middle finger longest, little finger low, the thumb on its
mound), cut the cream as its own shapes in the heel and across the finger bases, gave each finger its own
temperature, made the gouges swell and flick, shrank the sleeve to a cuff, and dropped the piece 2 cm.

Still weak: the thumb is a touch long and straight; at 9.5 cm the knuckle crescents are small (about 1.2 mm cuts,
they hold, but only just). The amber is shifted 2.6 units (about 0.9 mm) as one deliberate off-register.
