# 02 breath

**Placement:** collarbone, wearer's right, 17 cm wide, top 10.2 cm under the shoulder point, the newest breath just under the neck rib.
**Inks:** violet `#5B1F8E`, magenta `#A3217E`, amber `#F7C24D`, cream `#F6EFD2`.
**Words:** none.

## Brief

The back says "every breath rising into the December air". The front gives the wearer their own breath: three
exhales in one rising stream, leaving the collar and drifting up over the shoulder, three beats. The newest, nearest the neck, is small and
hot (cream and amber); the one before has risen, opened up and cooled to amber and magenta; the oldest is the
biggest, highest and cold, violet, tearing into two small puffs. Each breath is a cumulus of billows, each band its
own cut shape, warmest on the side it came from (the mouth, low and toward the neck), the edge always cooler. A few
of the billows' upper edges are echoed by a carved line, as a woodcut draws a cloud. No smoke curls, no gradient.

## How it pairs with the back

The back's breath is the crowd's, seen from the stage; the front's is yours, as if you just exhaled at the drop.
Alone it reads as warm breath on a cold night.

## Weak spots

Four draw rounds: a chain of flower-like puffs, then a smoke ribbon (read as a comet), then one cone of cloud (read
as rocket exhaust), then three separate breaths. The critic (one pass) said those still read as weather-icon clouds
stepping in a row, with carved arcs like a sleepy face, a dashed halo of slivers, and bands that were the same blob
shifted. The rework made one overlapping rising stream, larger; cut each warm band from its own billows, breaking
through to the breath's edge on the mouth side, with a wide violet rim on the cool side and one band (amber) stopped
by a straight knife cut; one long tapering carved line on each older breath, none on the newest; and stacked the
screens instead of trimming them, which removed the hairline gaps.

Still weak: the canvas can't rise above the neckline, so the stream climbs about 3 cm, less than breath really
would; at arm's length it can still be read as clouds before it reads as breath. It needs the back (or the cold) to
say "breath". The quietest of the eight.
