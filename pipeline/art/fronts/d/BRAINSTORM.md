# 04 map, bengaluru: fronts, round three

## What the front has to go with

The back is a printmaker's plan of the NICE grounds at the Bangalore International Exhibition Centre, Madavara, on
Tumakuru Road: the halls hatched at the top, the open ground in green with every person in the crowd drawn, the
Green Line's last stop at Madavara, the road jammed, the sound rings rolling out in blue over the village to
Anchepalya lake at the foot. Inks: key #22201D, grass green #2F7D52, lake blue #6E95CE, sound blue #2C46BE, on off
white #FFFAE7. The map is turned so north points up and 30° right; 100 m on it is 31.7 mm.

It is a big, busy, illustrative back. So the front is the opposite: small, quiet, mostly lettering, written in the
same language (the map's line, its inks, its note hand), never a piece of its picture.

## Who wears it

Mostly people who flew or drove in for the show: international, sophisticated, in love with Bengaluru's weather, its
trees, its night, its lakes. No rangoli, jasmine, bus boards, landmark clip-art, slogans, coordinates or tracklists
(LESSONS.md). Facts used below were checked in the last round's notes or here: Madavara is the end of the Green Line;
Anchepalya lake lies south past the village; the city sits on the Deccan plateau about 900 m up (no exact height was
found for the ground itself, so no number is drawn); 12 and 13 December 2026 are a Saturday and a Sunday; the moon
is a thin crescent that sets around nine, so after the show there is none; the rain tree folds its leaflets at dusk.

## What he liked, and Bengaluru's own version of it

- Mumbai's **map key**: a legend is what every printed map carries, and this shirt is a map too. Bengaluru's key gets
  its own entries (the halls, the ground, us, the last train, the lake, the sound), its own north (30° right) and
  scale, and a wordmark for a title. That is the closest one; the rest move further away.
- Mumbai's **looking up** (half circles over the skyline): Bengaluru's looking up is not a skyline. The map's own
  way of drawing "up" is a contour line: the plateau, rings that rhyme with the back's sound rings. Lying under a
  rain tree was tried too (its limbs fan out like rings from a stage) but at chest size it read as a tree logo; what
  stayed is the rain tree's own night detail, its folded leaf.
- Crowd's **filming**: the idea of one small honest object for the night. Here, the map's furniture plays that part:
  a north arrow, a scale bar, a sheet number, a leader line with a note.

## Ideas (17)

1. **The key.** A legend headed again.., six entries in the back's marks and note hand, north arrow at 30°, a scale
   bar true to the back. Left chest.
2. **The lake.** again.. drawn as an island, the lake's blue echo lines round it the way old maps draw a shore.
   Bengaluru is a city of tanks. Left chest.
3. **The plateau.** Contour lines of a low rise in green, the wordmark set in a clearing in them like a hill's name
   on a survey sheet, a trig point triangle at the top. Centre chest.
4. **Four scripts as place names.** again.., फिर से.., पुन्हा.., ಮತ್ತೆ.. lettered like names on a map: one spaced wide
   like a district, one along a road's curve, two small like villages with their dots. Not the stacked block. Centre
   chest.
5. **Two nights.** again.. with its two dots in blue, each dot pointed at by a leader line with a note: "sat 12",
   "sun 13". The two dots are the two nights. Left chest.
6. **The colophon.** A round map maker's seal: fred again.. round a ring, the north arrow at 30° inside, a small
   "bengaluru 2026". Left chest.
7. **Under the rain tree.** Looking up: a half circle of rain tree branches fanning from the trunk, the folded
   leaflets at their ends, and actual life under it. (Drawn; at chest size it read as a tree logo, not as looking
   up. Replaced by 7b.)
7b. **The folded leaf.** One rain tree leaf as a botanist draws it, its leaflets folded shut for the night, actual
   life under it. Left chest.
8. **The margin.** The line of small print in a map sheet's bottom margin: a ruled neat line with graticule ticks,
   "sheet 04", the four scripts in one row. A hem corner.
9. **Scale bar underline.** fred again.. in the note hand, underlined with a scale bar "0 50 100 m". (Folded into 1
   and 8.)
10. **You were here.** A map's position marker with "actual life". (Too close to a slogan.)
11. **The last train.** The Green Line's buffer stop at Madavara. (Already tried in round one and cut: it is a piece
    of the back.)
12. **Grid reference.** A map grid square "D 4" with one cell marked. (Clever, not felt.)
13. **The crescent.** A thin moon low in the west, setting at nine before the set, with again.. (Too close to
    Mumbai's sky; kept as a thought.)
14. **16° after midnight.** A weather station's note in the note hand. (A slogan in disguise.)
15. **The tank chain.** Bengaluru's lakes as a chain of small blue shapes linked by channels down a valley, with
    again.. (Too diagrammatic; the metro-line diagram was already thrown out.)
16. **The walk.** A dashed route from the station to the gate. (A piece of the back's picture.)
17. **Benchmark arrow.** The surveyor's broad arrow cut on a stone. (Most buyers won't know it.)

## Cut to two

The owner then asked for two fronts, done properly, instead of eight. Kept: **01 the key** (the richest, the
direction he liked made Bengaluru's own, many small true things) and **02 the lake** (a wordmark with one idea, the
furthest from a table). The others were drafted to round three and dropped.
