# 02 what the stage sees

**Placement:** full front, 33 cm wide (40 cm tall), top 9 cm under the shoulder point. **Inks:** cream line, yellow,
green, blue. **Words:** none.

**The idea.** The back is the crowd from behind, looking at the stage. This is the reverse shot, from the stage: the
lights are behind us, so the beams come over our heads and land on them, and every face is lit, drawn as a cream shape
with its features and its shadow side cut out of the light. Phones are seen from the back (camera bumps, torches on),
and one forearm rises out of the bottom edge, filming, its torch the yellow in the middle. Out past the crowd: the
sound-desk tent with two engineers and the two delay towers of an open-ground show.

**How it pairs.** It is literally the other side of the back. The people from the back are here, mirrored because we
are looking the other way: the girl in the yellow kurta on her friend's shoulders (back right, here left), the yellow
monkey cap (back left, here right). Front and back together put the wearer between the stage and the crowd.

**Critic pass (one).** Acted on: the sticker-like faces (now a cut shadow side on every lit face, varied expressions,
one man looking up into the beams mid-shout), the girl on the shoulders made bigger, the phone and torch brought into
the middle, the tent and towers 1.5x and the beams turned into long continuous strokes, the colour at the bottom
running out in an upward curve instead of a straight cut. Not acted on: wedge-shaped solid beams (they would read as
spotlights in a poster; the back's beams are pen strokes, so these are too).

**Weak spots.** The mid rows are still built from a shared face recipe and repeat at a glance; the shoulders couple is
small for its story; it is the heaviest of the eight and will feel like a second back to some.
